01 — About

Representation,
interpretation, decision.

A recurring theme in my work is that computational systems do not describe the world neutrally — they structure how it is interpreted. That holds whether the system is a routing model over a street network, an interface guiding a novice programmer, or a neural network inferring light in a scene.

Networks are my strongest intellectual interest: the way physical, social, infrastructural, and digital networks overlap, and what is gained or lost when messy spatial and social relationships are translated into graphs, routes, and optimization problems.

I approach this from a liberal arts perspective rather than treating computation as a purely technical exercise. I care about who a dataset represents, whose experience gets abstracted away, and how design choices shape the decisions people are able to make. I don’t see technical rigor and social awareness as competing goals — the strongest work needs both.
